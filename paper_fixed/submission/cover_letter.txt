Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript "Obstruction, Chain Templating, and the
Limits of Janus Face Selectivity at a WSSe Monolayer in PEO/LiTFSI
Composite Polymer Electrolytes: An Atomistic Molecular Dynamics Study
with Causal and Null Controls" for consideration as a research article in
the Journal of Molecular Liquids.

The work is an all-atom molecular dynamics study of a Janus WSSe
monolayer embedded in a molten poly(ethylene oxide)/LiTFSI electrolyte at
400 K, above the PEO melting point, paired with a filler-free reference
system and a battery of controls (a null chemistry control, a causal
chemistry-swap control, and two independently re-packed replicas) built
to test, rather than assume, the causal origin of any interfacial
signal.

The principal, controlled result is that the WSSe sheet obstructs
transport and templates polymer structure. Against a matched filler-free
system, in-plane Li+ and TFSI- mobility fall by 24% and 18%
respectively while the Li-O coordination shell is unchanged, showing
that the effect is structural obstruction rather than a chemical
perturbation; PEO chains template parallel to the sheet within ~7 A
where the control's own slab interior stays flat, confirming genuine
filler-induced templating rather than a free-surface artefact. The
sheet spans the full periodic cross-section of the cell, so cross-plane
transport is not merely slowed but arrested, with a net of
approximately one Li+ traversing the sheet in 30 ns.

We also tested, and did not confirm, a face-selectivity hypothesis
motivated by gas-phase DFT studies on Janus transition-metal
dichalcogenides, which report that the Se-terminated face binds
adsorbates more strongly than the S face in vacuum. A single production
trajectory shows a Se-face enrichment of up to 1.5x (3.4-5.0 sigma), but
this does not survive the control battery: a null control where both
faces are made chemically identical reproduces enrichment ratios
(1.06-1.16) comparable in size to the published effect; a causal control
that swaps which face carries which chemistry leaves the enrichment on
the same geometric face rather than following the swapped chemistry; and
two independent repacked replicas disagree with each other on the sign
of the effect. We report this as a tested and falsified hypothesis,
together with the control battery that falsifies it, rather than as a
confirmed result -- both because it is the honest conclusion and because
it is, we believe, a useful worked example of why a single-trajectory
face-population count on a thermally corrugated, finite-sampled 2D sheet
needs a null and a causal control before being reported as a chemical
effect.

Two further results corroborate the simulation's reliability
independently of either claim above: the anion-to-cation diffusivity
ratio D_TFSI/D_Li = 2.65 and the ideal Nernst-Einstein transference
number t+ = 0.27 reproduce the known behaviour of neat PEO/LiTFSI and
validate the charge-scaled force field.

I have been explicit about the limits of the study. The monolayer spans
the full periodic cross-section of the simulation cell, so it represents
the complete-blocking limit rather than a dispersed composite, and no
out-of-plane diffusion coefficient is defined for this geometry; the
manuscript states this and reports cross-plane blocking directly instead.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not
under consideration by another journal. I am the sole author and have no
competing interests to declare.
